To follow up first time visitors well, collect their name and phone number on the day, have a real person contact them within a few days, invite them to one specific next step, and track each visitor until they either settle into the church or tell you they are not looking for a church home. Most visitors who never return were not rejected; they were simply forgotten in the busy week after Sunday. A small, consistent system fixes that.

## Why visitor follow up matters

A first time visitor has already done the hardest part. They chose your church, found the building and walked through the door. Some came because a friend invited them, some have just moved to the area, and some are going through a difficult season and looking for help.

What happens in the following week often decides whether they come back. A warm, personal contact tells them they were noticed and that they are welcome. Silence tells them the opposite, even if the service itself was wonderful.

## Step 1: Collect the right details on the day

You cannot follow up someone you have no way to reach. Make capturing details part of how you welcome visitors, not an awkward form at the end.

Ask for:

- **Name**
- **Phone number**, preferably one they use on WhatsApp
- **Area where they live**, so you can connect them with a nearby cell group
- **How they heard about the church**, such as a friend, social media, a crusade or simply walking past
- **Whether they would like prayer or a visit**

Ushers or a welcome team can collect this on a short card or straight onto a phone. Thank each visitor personally during or after the service, but avoid putting anyone on the spot in front of the whole congregation if they seem shy.

Always ask permission before adding a visitor to a WhatsApp group or mailing list. Ghana's Data Protection Act expects organisations to collect personal information for a clear purpose and handle it carefully, and visitors appreciate being asked.

## Step 2: Make personal contact within days

Speed matters, but personal matters more. A good rhythm for many churches is:

- **Sunday evening or Monday:** a short, warm WhatsApp or SMS message thanking them for coming, ideally from the pastor or the welcome team leader.
- **By midweek:** a phone call from someone on the follow up team, simply to say thank you, ask how they found the service and whether there is anything the church can pray about.
- **Before the next Sunday:** an invitation to come again, mentioning a specific service or programme.

Keep the first conversation light. The goal is not to sign them up for everything; it is to let them know someone cares.

## Step 3: Assign every visitor to a named person

When follow up is "everyone's job", it quietly becomes nobody's job. Give each visitor a named person responsible for them. Where possible, match visitors with someone of a similar age, stage of life or area, so the conversation feels natural.

Set a follow up date for each visitor, so it is clear when the next call or visit should happen, and keep short notes after every conversation. If one team member travels or falls ill, someone else can pick up where they left off without asking the visitor the same questions again.

## Step 4: Invite them to one clear next step

"Come again sometime" is easy to ignore. A specific invitation is not. Depending on the visitor, that might be:

- A newcomers' class or welcome lunch
- A home cell or fellowship meeting near where they live
- A department that fits their interests, such as the choir, ushering, media or the youth ministry
- A special programme coming up in the next few weeks

People stay in churches where they have relationships and a place to contribute. Connecting visitors with a small group early is one of the strongest ways to help them stay.

## Step 5: Track every visitor through clear stages

A simple set of stages keeps the whole team on the same page:

1. **Pending:** visited, but no one has made contact yet
2. **Contacted:** someone has called or met them
3. **Revisited:** they came back to church
4. **Converted:** they have joined the church and are settling in

Reviewing this list each week shows who still needs a call and which visitors are slipping away. It also reveals patterns. If many visitors are contacted but few come back, the problem may be the invitation or the welcome on their second visit, not the phone calls.

## Step 6: Review the numbers every month

Once a month, the pastor and welcome team should look at a few simple questions:

- How many visitors came this month, and through which channel did they hear about us?
- How many were contacted within the first week?
- How many came back for a second visit?
- How many have joined a cell group or department?

You do not need complicated reports. Even these four numbers will show you where your follow up is strong and where visitors are falling through the gaps.

## Common follow up mistakes

- Collecting details but never calling
- Leaving all follow up to the senior pastor
- Adding visitors to large WhatsApp groups without asking
- Only contacting visitors when there is a fundraising appeal
- Losing visitor cards because they were never entered anywhere
