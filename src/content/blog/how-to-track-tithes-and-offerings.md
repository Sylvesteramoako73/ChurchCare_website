The simplest reliable way to track tithes and offerings is this: count every collection with at least two people before it leaves the room, record each gift against a named member (or as general giving), note whether it came in cash, Mobile Money or bank transfer, reconcile your Mobile Money and bank statements every week, and give members a receipt or statement for what they gave. Everything else in this guide is about making those five habits easy enough that your team keeps them up every Sunday.

## Why careful giving records matter

Members give in faith, and they deserve to know their giving is handled faithfully. Clear records protect your counting team from suspicion, give your finance committee accurate numbers for budgeting, and make it possible to answer simple questions quickly, such as how much came in for the building project this quarter, or whether tithes are growing or falling.

Good records also make life easier for members. When someone asks for a statement of what they gave this year, you should be able to produce it in minutes, not spend an evening searching through envelopes and notebooks.

## Step 1: Separate the types of giving

Decide on a short, fixed list of giving types and use the same names every time. A typical list for a Ghanaian church looks like this:

- **Tithe**, the tenth that members bring regularly
- **Offering**, the general collection during service
- **Seed**, special faith offerings
- **Project or building fund**, giving towards a named project
- **Other**, such as thanksgiving or welfare, if you track those separately

Keep the list short. If every department invents its own category, your reports quickly become impossible to compare from one month to the next.

## Step 2: Agree a counting procedure

Most problems with church money start with an unclear counting process. Write down a simple procedure and follow it every week:

1. At least two people count together, ideally not related to each other.
2. Count in a closed room straight after the collection, before anyone leaves.
3. Fill in a count sheet showing cash by denomination, total for each giving type and the names on tithe envelopes.
4. Both counters sign the count sheet.
5. The cash goes into the bank, or into the church safe, the same day or the next working day.

Rotate counters regularly. Rotation is not a sign of distrust; it simply makes the system stronger and protects the people doing the work.

## Step 3: Capture Mobile Money and bank giving properly

More and more members give by Mobile Money or bank transfer. These gifts are easy to lose track of, because they do not pass through the counting room.

A few habits help a lot:

- **Use one dedicated number.** Give members a single church Mobile Money number or merchant code, rather than the personal number of a pastor or treasurer.
- **Ask for a reference.** Encourage members to add their name and the giving type in the reference, for example "Ama Owusu tithe".
- **Download statements weekly.** Each week, download the Mobile Money and bank statements and match every incoming payment to a giver and a giving type.
- **Record the payment method.** When you record the gift, note whether it was cash, Mobile Money or bank transfer. At the end of the month, the Mobile Money total in your records should match the statement.

## Step 4: Record each gift against the right member

Tithe records only become useful when each gift is linked to the right person. Common names make this harder than it sounds; most churches have more than one Kwame Mensah or Akosua Boateng.

Give every member a unique membership number and use it on tithe envelopes and in your records. When a gift cannot be linked to a person, record it as general giving rather than guessing.

## Step 5: Give receipts and statements

Receipts build trust. They show members that their gift arrived and was recorded correctly. At minimum:

- Offer a receipt for any tithe or project gift on request.
- Send an SMS receipt for Mobile Money gifts if you can, so members know the payment was received.
- Provide a yearly giving statement to any member who asks for one.

If a member questions an amount, the receipt and the signed count sheet let you resolve it calmly and quickly.

## Step 6: Reconcile and report every month

Once a month, the treasurer should bring together the count sheets, bank deposits, Mobile Money statements and the giving records, and confirm that they agree. Then prepare a short report for the pastors and finance committee showing:

- Total giving for the month, by type
- Totals by payment method
- Comparison with the previous month and the same month last year
- Progress on any project or building fund pledges

A one page report read every month is far more useful than a detailed report that only appears at the end of the year.

## Step 7: Decide who can see giving records

Giving is personal. Only the people who need to see individual giving, usually the treasurer, finance team and senior pastor, should have access to it. Department heads may need attendance and contact details, but not what each member gives.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Counting alone, or leaving the counting until later in the week
- Mixing tithe and offering into one total
- Collecting Mobile Money on personal phones
- Recording gifts by first name only
- Keeping the only copy of the records in one notebook or on one laptop
- Waiting until December to reconcile the year
